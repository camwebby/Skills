# Red-Team Checklist

The red-team pass tries to disprove the idea. It should be adversarial, specific, and evidence-aware.

## Red-Team Roles

### Customer Skeptic
Attack: the pain is not severe, frequent, or urgent enough.
Questions:
- What behavior proves customers care now?
- What do they do today when this problem happens?
- Is the problem painful enough to change behavior?

### Buyer and Budget Skeptic
Attack: the user likes it, but the buyer will not pay.
Questions:
- Who owns the budget?
- What budget line does this replace or expand?
- Does the buyer already spend money on this job?

### Incumbent Defender
Attack: existing tools can copy, bundle, discount, or block this.
Questions:
- Why would users switch from existing workflows?
- What distribution, data, or workflow advantage can the incumbent exploit?
- Is the wedge too small or too easy to copy?

### GTM Skeptic
Attack: acquisition is too expensive, slow, or noisy.
Questions:
- Where can the team reach qualified buyers repeatedly?
- What is the likely sales cycle?
- What channel has evidence, not just hope?

### Pricing Skeptic
Attack: value exists but cannot be monetized.
Questions:
- Is the value tied to a budgeted outcome?
- Does pricing scale with value delivered?
- Will support or implementation erase margins?

### Operational Skeptic
Attack: delivery breaks under real customer conditions.
Questions:
- What manual work hides behind the product promise?
- What quality, support, or onboarding burden appears at scale?
- What dependency fails if usage grows?

### Trust, Security, and Data Skeptic
Attack: users will not grant the access needed to deliver value.
Questions:
- What sensitive data, permissions, or integrations are required?
- What buyer objections will security, legal, or IT raise?
- Can value be delivered without privileged access?

### Regulatory or Platform-Risk Skeptic
Attack: laws, platforms, marketplaces, or policy changes can kill this.
Questions:
- Is the business dependent on one platform, API, rule, or data source?
- Are there licensing, privacy, consumer protection, healthcare, finance, labor, or education constraints?
- What breaks if regulation tightens?

### Founder-Fit Skeptic
Attack: the idea may be good, but this team may not be advantaged.
Questions:
- Does the team have access to buyers?
- Does the team understand the workflow deeply?
- What hard part is the team unusually good at?

## Red-Team Output Format

| Failure mode | Red-team role | Severity | Likelihood | Why it might happen | Early warning signal | Falsification test |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|

End with:
- strongest case against the idea
- single highest-risk assumption
- what evidence would reverse the objection
